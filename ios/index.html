iOS simulators

Some screens can only be seen in the running app: real navigation, sheets, Liquid Glass, data from your server. For these, facetshot opens the screen in the iOS simulator by deep link and takes the simulator’s screenshot. For layout checks that don’t need the app running, SwiftUI scenes are much faster.

iOS targets need a Mac with Xcode.

Setup

Section titled “Setup”
  • scheme is the URL scheme your app handles. app:/inbox/42 opens myapp:///inbox/42; set deepLinkStyle: 'host' if your app expects myapp://inbox/42.
  • ios.build builds the app when its inputs change. heavy: true means only one such build runs on the machine at a time. Built apps are also kept in ~/.facetshot/cache/apps/, keyed by their inputs, so another checkout of the same code doesn’t build again. If you build the app yourself, give its path as ios.app instead.
  • env is passed to the app at launch. Placeholders work, so the app can find a server facetshot started.

What happens

Section titled “What happens”
  1. The first time it needs them, facetshot makes simulators of its own, such as facetshot-iphone-17-pro-max. It never boots, changes or erases a simulator it didn’t make.
  2. It boots them (at most two at a time), installs the app if it changed, sets the status bar to 9:41 with full signal bars, and launches the app once per simulator.
  3. For each variant it changes the simulator’s appearance, text size and contrast while the app runs. For each screen it opens the deep link and waits until the screen is ready.
  4. It takes the simulator’s screenshot.

While there are only a few devices, each gets a simulator of its own, so the iPad in portrait and in landscape are taken in parallel, and a simulator that finishes early hands its slot to the next. Opening deep links, rotating and playing flow steps go through a small UI-test runner that facetshot builds once and keeps.

When a screen counts as ready

Section titled “When a screen counts as ready”

Without help from your app, facetshot waits until two screenshots in a row, a quarter of a second apart, are the same (for up to 8 seconds). That works, but your app knows better when it has finished loading. With readyFile: true, facetshot gives the app a path in FACETSHOT_READY_FILE. Write any file there once the screen has its data and has nothing left to load, and facetshot takes the screenshot right away (after a short wait for animations to stop):

facetshot removes the file before every screen, so write it again after each navigation.

Variants on iOS

Section titled “Variants on iOS”
Variant How
light, dark the simulator’s appearance, changed while the app runs
large, xlarge, xxl Dynamic Type: accessibility medium, extra large and extra-extra-extra large
contrast Increase Contrast

Locale, right to left, personas, offline and reduced motion aren’t applied on the simulator yet; the screenshot’s notes say so. If you need them now, pass what your app needs through env and variants of your own.

iPad in Split View

Section titled “iPad in Split View”

The ipad-half device takes the app at half the screen’s width. A simulator can’t be put into Split View from outside, so facetshot tells the app the width in points with FACETSHOT_SPLIT_WIDTH at launch, and crops the screenshot to that half. To support this, lay the app out at that width in debug builds. The screenshot’s notes say that the app drew itself at that width.

Flows

Section titled “Flows”

On the simulator, goto and shot work through deep links, and the other steps through the runner (a small UI test facetshot builds once). Start the flow with start: app:/….

Step On the simulator
click, tap a tap
longpress, rightclick a press held for a second, which opens an element’s context menu
drag press, hold, and move to the second element or point
fill, type a tap on the field, then typing
wait, gone until the element is there, or gone
scroll by pixels one swipe up or down

An element is named by its accessibility label or identifier (text=Save, #save, or the label alone), role=button[name="Save"], or by a point on the screen in points (@120,300). A role is matched against the element’s type, named the way the web names roles (button, textbox, switch, cell, text…). Which steps aren’t available on the simulator is in Flows on apps. Finding elements by role or point, long presses, drags and the log below are new and not yet run on a simulator (Status).

Logs

Section titled “Logs”

Each screenshot carries the lines the app wrote to the system log while it was on screen (Logger, os_log, NSLog), with the limits in Logs.

Each screen starts where the last one left off

Section titled “Each screen starts where the last one left off”

The app is launched once per simulator, and screens are opened one after another by deep link. If opening a deep link in your app doesn’t close a sheet or alert that the previous screen left open, the next screenshot shows that sheet. That is usually a real bug (a user tapping a link would see the same), and the screenshot makes it visible.
